# The Ninth Lantern — story bible (for the book's authors, not compiled)

This is the shared reference for the example manuscript that *A Book, Start to
Finish* follows. Every chapter of the book demonstrates an Inkhaven feature by
using it on THIS story, so the names, world, and plot must stay consistent
across chapters. Keep the story light — it is a scaffold for the features, not
a novel to finish. Use only what a chapter needs.

## Logline
In the harbour town of **Saltmarch**, nine lanterns have burned without fail for
three hundred years. One cold morning the ninth is dark and its keeper is gone —
and a young under-keeper who goes looking for him uncovers what the light was
really holding back.

## Setting
- **Saltmarch** — a small, poor fishing town on a cold northern coast; stone
  quays, a Long Mole reaching into the harbour, fog off the water most mornings.
- **The nine lanterns** — great oil lanterns on stone pillars ringing the
  harbour; the ninth stands alone at the end of the Long Mole. The keepers'
  creed: *no lantern goes dark*. The town believes the light keeps the
  **sea-fret** (a cold fog off the water) from coming ashore.
- **The light-magic (soft, understated)** — the light does hold the fret back;
  what the town does not remember is *why the fret wants in*. Three hundred years
  ago Saltmarch did something it buried; the lanterns are the bargain that kept
  it buried, and the bargain is ending.

## The inciting incident
On the morning the story opens, the **ninth lantern is cold** and its keeper,
**Aldous Crane**, is not at his post. He did not vanish: he put the lantern out
himself and walked out along the Mole into the fret — because he had learned what
the light really holds back.

## Cast
- **Mira Fenn** — the protagonist. Young under-keeper, apprenticed to the
  harbourmaster; practical, stubborn, the one who finds the cold lantern and
  goes looking. POV character.
- **Aldous Crane** — keeper of the ninth lantern; old, solitary, and the one who
  knows the secret. Missing from the opening.
- **Sella Vale** — the harbourmaster; Mira's stern mentor; keeper of the town's
  official story.
- **Toft** — the lamp-oil merchant; a plausible early suspect (a cold lantern
  looks like a supply he failed to deliver).
- **Bryn Crane** — Aldous's estranged nephew, back in town; knows more of the
  family's past than he says.

## The secret (used for the KEN / who-knows-what chapter)
The ninth lantern was **put out on purpose, by Aldous himself.** Early on it
looks like an accident or a failure of oil; the truth — that Aldous extinguished
it deliberately, and why — is the mid-book reveal. So: no character should speak
or act on "Aldous put the lantern out himself" *before* they could have learned
it. That is exactly the kind of premature-knowledge slip the who-knows-what
reader (KEN) guards, and the chapter uses it.

## The shape (for the read-through / revision chapters)
A short forward arc: the cold lantern (opening) → suspicion falls the wrong way
(Toft) → Mira follows Aldous's trail onto the Mole → the reveal of what the light
holds back → the choice of whether to relight it. Kishōtenketsu or a simple
three-act reading both fit; the read-through chapter measures the realised shape
against one of these.

## Non-fiction parallel
Wherever a step is fiction-specific (the world, the cast, who-knows-what, the
voices), the chapter names the parallel for a non-fiction author — e.g. the
Facts book holds a novel's world OR a non-fiction book's verified claims; the
read-through's "confusion" flag serves an argument as well as a plot.
