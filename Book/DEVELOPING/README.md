# Developing a story with Inkhaven

*An Author's Guide to Every Kind of Book — Choosing a Track and Building It.*

A process guide to using Inkhaven for whatever you are actually writing. Inkhaven
is one program but many tools — a structured editor, a world simulation, a
constructed-language workshop, a research assistant, a family of AI readers, a
citation manager, and a press. This book answers the question the feature manuals
don't: **which of those tools are mine, and in what order, for the book I am
writing?**

It sorts the work Inkhaven supports into eight **tracks** and gives each a full
working guide:

- **Fiction** — an invented world held consistent.
- **Utopia** — the society as an argument, kept coherent.
- **Science fiction** — invention that must obey a declared rule.
- **Nonfiction** — the claim that must be true.
- **Scenarios** — the usable world others will play in.
- **Technical documentation** — the text that must match a changing system.
- **Scientific writing** — the argument that must be sourced and survive review.
- **Theology & philosophy** — the position argued in good faith.

Each guide runs the same **loop** — frame, gather, draft, read, revise — tailored
to its track, tying structure, worldbuilding, research, and the AI readers into
one process rather than a drawer of gadgets.

The first two chapters are for everyone: a side-by-side comparison of the tracks
(and the one `genre` setting that tunes everything), and a full tour of the desk —
navigation, editing, the companion books, snapshots, split-edit, and export.

**Visual style and author voice follow *Building the World with Inkhaven*** — the
same warm palette, the same clearly-marked asides: **Note** (how Inkhaven behaves),
**Insight** (a principle to remember), **Ask Yourself** (a question about *your*
book), **Pitfall** (a mistake to avoid), and **Try It** (an exercise at the
keyboard).

It is a companion to the three deep manuals — *Building the World with Inkhaven*,
*Constructed Language Development with Inkhaven*, and *The Research Assistant with
Inkhaven* — and points you to them where a track needs their depth.
