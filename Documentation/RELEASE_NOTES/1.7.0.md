# Inkhaven 1.7.0 — The Linguist's Desk

_The 1.7 flagship opens: a full-spectrum linguistic layer for the constructed
languages a book invents. This first cut is the companion and its first analysis
instruments — a grounded workspace over the Language book, and two deterministic
passes that measure a language against itself and against the typological
baseline. No new runtime crates._

## A companion for your languages

`inkhaven linguistic` launches a new full-screen TUI — sibling to `inkhaven
research`, and built on the same shell. It opens over the **Language system
book**: the tree of your constructed languages on the left, and a right pane that
cycles (Tab) through four views —

- **Preview** — the selected node's content.
- **Chat** — a streaming, **grounded** conversation with the Inner Linguist. Each
  answer is retrieved (via the same book-RAG the Research companion uses) from the
  language sub-book that contains your selection, so it reasons about *your*
  phonology, lexicon and grammar — not a generic language. It answers in the
  project language.
- **Metrics** (`m`) and **Universals** (`u`) — the two analysis passes below.

The chat is a named, resumable **session** (`--session <name>`): the transcript
persists to `.inkhaven/linguistic-sessions/` and replays when you reopen it.
`--language <name>` opens focused on one language.

## Metrics — a language measured against itself

`inkhaven language metrics <lang>` (and the TUI's Metrics view) reports the
quantitative, information-theoretic complement to `language stats`:

- **Phoneme entropy** — Shannon H of the phoneme distribution, its maximum,
  evenness, and perplexity (the effective inventory size).
- **Zipf fit** — the least-squares slope + R² of the phoneme rank-frequency
  (≈ −1 is Zipfian).
- **Phonotactic saturation** — attested vs possible syllable types: how densely
  the lexicon fills its own phonotactic grid.
- **Mora weight** — mean moras per word and the heavy-syllable ratio.

All deterministic and corpus-free — computed from the dictionary forms that parse
as the language's own phonemes.

## Universals — a language against the typological baseline

`inkhaven language universals <lang>` (and the TUI's Universals view) judges your
grammar block against how the world's languages actually behave:

- **Head-directionality harmony** — whether your adposition, genitive and
  relative-clause orders branch the same way as your verb phrase (Dryer's
  correlation-pair generalisation), naming any disharmonic outliers.
- **Implicational universals** — Greenberg's 2, 3 and 4 and the OV↔GenN / OV↔RelN
  correlations, each judged *satisfied*, *violated*, or *not applicable*.
- **Word order + morphotype** — a one-line survey.

A violation is a **flag, not an error** — natural languages break tendencies too;
the point is to show you where your language sits and what is unusual. The
typological-feature catalogue grew from 16 to 22 features to support it.

## Under the hood

This release also completed the pre-1.7 groundwork it stands on: the shared
`tui_host` companion shell and `system_tree` pane (so the Linguistic companion is
not a fork of the Research one), a hardening pass on the language suite, and a
split of the 9.3 k-line `cli/language` handler into sixteen per-family submodules
— so the analysis verbs have a home to grow into.

## Dependencies & compatibility

**No new runtime crates.** Everything is additive: a new `linguistic` command and
two new `language` subcommands (`metrics`, `universals`); existing projects,
languages and scripts are unchanged. Warning-free (binary and tests). Test suite
→ 2484.

This is the first slice of the 1.7 layer; the analysis Oracle, the feature-matrix
metrics, IGT + hypothesis instruments, and the Inner Linguist reader follow in
later 1.7.x releases.
