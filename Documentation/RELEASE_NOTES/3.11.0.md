# Inkhaven 3.11.0 — The Canon Ledger

_The flagship is a new axis: inkhaven now remembers **the decisions behind the
fiction**, not just the text (git) or the current state (Facts / SEMNET / KEN /
SENTINEL) or the readers' findings over time (CHRONICLE). The **Canon Ledger** is
the development history of your story's canon — every load-bearing decision, what
it rests on, how settled it is, and where it came from — so you can finally ask
"what breaks if I cut this?" Built on the [`smysl`](https://github.com/vulogov/smysl)
crate (1.7), which inkhaven's own SMYSL-1 RFC drove the features into._

Full guide: [`CANON.md`](../CANON.md). Deterministic core, warning-free.

# The Canon Ledger

Each load-bearing decision — a **world-fact**, **character trait**, **plot point**,
**reveal**, or **setup** — is recorded with its **grounds** (what it depends on), a
**commitment** level (`floated → drafted → committed → canonical → retconned` —
authorial canonicity, not real-world truth), and a link back to the **source
paragraph**. Identity is a content hash, so the same decision recorded twice is
idempotent and independent sessions reconcile by merge.

## What it answers

- **"What breaks if I cut this?"** — `canon impact <id>` reports the blast radius:
  every decision that transitively rests on the one you're removing. Nothing in
  inkhaven did dependency-aware revision impact before.
- **"Why is this canon?"** — `canon why <id>` traces the grounds chain.
- **"How settled is it, and am I building on sand?"** — `canon commit` sets a
  decision's commitment; `canon check` flags anything committed *above* the weakest
  thing it rests on (a canonical scene resting on a floated premise).
- **"Where do agents disagree?"** — merge two ledgers and `canon forks` surfaces
  concurrent disagreements on canonicity, recorded rather than silently resolved.
- **Grounded answers** — `canon context "<query>"` fits the relevant decisions and
  their grounds/rebuttals to a token budget for an LLM, closure-complete.

## How decisions enter — with the author always in control

- **Deterministic harvest on save** turns authored tags (e.g. a `rel:` relationship
  tag) into decisions, at no cost; untagged paragraphs produce nothing.
- **Opt-in LLM harvest** (`canon harvest`) *proposes* decisions from prose in your
  project language, staged in `.inkhaven/canon-staged.json`; **nothing enters the
  ledger until you `canon accept`**. The model proposes; you decide.

## In the editor

The **reader hub** (`Ctrl+B *`) gains **Canon** — a dashboard of every decision
with its kind and commitment, plus a commitment-forks section; `Enter` jumps to a
decision's source paragraph.

## Why it's cheap and safe

The deterministic queries (`impact` / `why` / `list` / `check` / `forks`) are pure
functions over a small per-project store — no model call, milliseconds at book
scale. The ledger is a **derived** artifact: a lost `canon.cbor` costs at most a
re-harvest, never prose. The one model path (harvest) is opt-in and never writes
without confirmation. The `smysl` dependency is the pure profile — no async, no
HTTP, no C/C++, no new heavy transitive tree.

## Upgrade notes

Nothing to migrate — the ledger starts empty and populates as you save tagged
paragraphs or run `canon harvest`. All of it is reachable today from the
`inkhaven canon …` CLI and the reader-hub dashboard.
