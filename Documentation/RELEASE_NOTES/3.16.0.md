# Inkhaven 3.16.0 — The Ledger Reads Back · The Rest of the Word

_Through 3.15 the Canon Ledger only answered: what breaks if I cut this, why is
this canon, how settled is it. 3.16.0 makes it speak first. The ledger now reads
itself against the manuscript and reports where the two have come apart — as a
reader, alongside SENTINEL, KEN and BONDS, in the same worklist, history and
dashboards. Deterministic and free at its core; one opt-in model pass for the
case counting cannot see._

_The second track finishes what 3.15's world work deferred: a regional map gets a
latitude, the world's proposals are written in your book's language, the compile
stops freezing the editor, and the world store learns to change shape. No new
store, no new dependency; warning-free._

Full guides: [`CANON.md`](../CANON.md) · [`WORLDBUILDING.md`](../WORLDBUILDING.md).

## `canon read` — the four free findings

```
inkhaven canon read [<scope>] [--json] [--strict] [--deep] [--max-cost N]
```

| Finding | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Drifted from its source** | The paragraph that established a decision no longer mentions any of it (stemmed, in the project language; a `rel:`-tagged decision is carried by its tag). |
| **Orphaned** | That paragraph no longer exists. |
| **Built on sand** | Committed above something it rests on. |
| **Unsettled foundation** | Two or more decisions rest on one still unmarked or floated — reported once you have committed something. |

`<scope>` narrows the report to a chapter or paragraph; `--json` is for scripts;
`--strict` exits non-zero when there is anything to report. `canon check` is
unchanged.

## The contradiction pass — opt-in

`canon read --deep`, or **`D`** on the Canon dashboard, asks a model **once**
whether any passage of the book says the *opposite* of a decision you marked
committed or canonical. Passages are retrieved locally; everything goes in one
prompt fitted to the budget (`--max-cost`, or `canon.deep_budget` in the editor,
both 8000 tokens by default) — decisions that do not fit are reported as
unchecked, the run is never refused. The model must cite the decision and the
passage; a citation it was not offered is dropped.

The answer is stored, so every later read, the Editorial Pass, CHRONICLE and the
dashboard show it without another call — and a stored finding **expires on its
own** when the passage changes or the decision is no longer committed.

## Where the findings appear

- **Canon dashboard** — findings listed first; `Enter` opens the paragraph
  involved, `c` / `g` / `h` act on the decision.
- **Canon pane** — a marker under the decision (`⚠ drifted`, `⚠ on sand`, …).
- **Reader hub** — Canon shows its count beside the other readers.
- **Editorial Pass / `inkhaven revise`** — under the source `canon`. *Drifted* and
  *contradicted* are **Decisions** (the scene or the ledger — you say which); the
  rest are **Briefs** (you reconcile the ledger; no prose is touched).
- **CHRONICLE** — counted in every milestone, so the trend shows whether the
  ledger and the book are converging.
- **Bund** — `ink.canon.read ( -- list )`, read-only.

## The world track — the rest of the word

**A regional map has a latitude.** A heightmap with a scale was measured as a
region but still weathered pole to pole. Add `dem.center_lat` (and optionally
`center_lon`) and its rows span the latitudes its height really covers: a 1200 km
map at 10°N is tropical from edge to edge, the same map at 80°N is cold
throughout. Climate, `weather`, the scene brief, `set-coords --lat/--lon` and
landmarks given in degrees all read the same band. Opt-in — a world without the
key compiles to the same output as 3.15.0; `validate` tells you when a regional
map has no latitude.

**Proposals in your book's language.** Places, rulers, language briefs, Mythology
entries and `critique` Notes follow the project language (en / ru / fr / de / es) —
both the line you read in the queue and what is committed on accept. The
vocabulary is translated, not just the sentence: settlement class, siting, the
twelve biomes, the generated ethos and beliefs, the language-profile terms.

```
Korason — город с населением около 22944 человек. Расположение: в устье реки.
Природная зона: умеренные степи.
```

A belief or ethos you declared is left as you wrote it, and names are never
translated. A Mythology symbol's vocabulary is in the prose language, because
`myth scan` looks for those words in the manuscript; it matches whole words, so
the Russian and German seeds carry the commonest case forms — a starting list to
extend. A belief declared in a non-Latin script now yields a vocabulary (it used
to yield nothing).

**The compile runs in the background.** `Ctrl+B W` → `C` used to compile every
layer, write and re-embed the World book and seed the queue on the UI thread. It
now runs off it: the status line names each layer, and you can keep writing and
saving. `C` again cancels between layers; what was written stays and the next `C`
finishes. The compiler owns the World book's leaves — don't edit one while it
runs.

**Decisions that belong to their world.** A place proposal is identified by its
map cell, so an accept or reject now applies under the seed it was made under:
change the seed and the new world's towns are offered afresh. Rulers, languages
and myths are identified by name and stay settled. And coordinates you set with
`realworld set-coords` are no longer moved by a map render, which refines only
positions that came from the compiler or an earlier render.

## Upgrade notes

**The world store upgrades itself, one way.** The first time 3.16.0 opens a
project, `.inkhaven/world.db` gains two columns; nothing is rewritten or lost.
After that, an older inkhaven refuses the file and asks you to upgrade — keep
that in mind if you share a project between machines. Place decisions made
before the upgrade carry no seed and keep applying under every seed.

A non-English project will see its *pending* proposals rewritten in its own
language the next time it proposes; paragraphs already accepted stay as they
were, and `critique` writes fresh Notes rather than refreshing English-titled
ones.

The canon side has nothing to migrate. The four free findings appear as soon as
you open the Canon dashboard or run `canon read`; if a long-lived ledger reports many *drifted*
decisions, that is the reader doing its job on prose that moved — each is a
choice, not an error, and can be deferred in the Editorial Pass like any finding.
The contradiction pass never runs unless you ask for it.
