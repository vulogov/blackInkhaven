# Inkhaven 3.12.0 — Grounds That Hold

_3.11.0 shipped the Canon Ledger — decisions behind the fiction, with the flagship
question **"what breaks if I cut this?"** (`canon impact`). But that question walks
a **dependency graph**, and nothing populated it: every decision entered with no
grounds, so `impact` and `why` came back empty. 3.12.0 makes the graph real — and
adds the development-history view the ledger was pitched for, plus the pre-cut
guard. Deterministic core, warning-free._

Full guide: [`CANON.md`](../CANON.md). Builds on
[`smysl`](https://github.com/vulogov/smysl) (pure profile, no new heavy deps).

---

## The dependency graph now populates itself

A decision's **grounds** — what it rests on — get drawn three ways, and you rarely
draw them by hand:

- **Deterministically, free.** When a decision enters the ledger, its grounds are
  inferred from **kind rules + shared content words**: a *reveal* rests on the
  *setup* it shares a word with; a *plot-point* / *reveal* rests on the *world-fact*
  whose subject it reuses. Matching is stemmed and stop-word-filtered in your
  project language (so "leviathan" ~ "leviathan's", and function words never
  match). Conservative by design — a spurious ground would distort `impact`.
- **By the opt-in harvest.** The harvest model, already reading a scene, now names
  which sibling decisions each one rests on; those are resolved on `canon accept`
  and unioned with the deterministic edges. Still staged, still author-confirmed —
  `canon staged` shows the proposed grounds before anything enters.
- **By hand.** `canon ground <id> --on <ground>` draws an edge the harvest missed;
  `canon unground <id> --from <ground>` removes a wrong one. In the editor, **`g`**
  in the Canon dashboard grounds the cursored decision on a target you pick.

So `canon impact` and `canon why` finally return real answers on a harvested
project. Grounds are part of a decision's content hash, so editing them mints a new
version that **supersedes** the old and relinks everything that rested on it — the
history is kept, and a decision's **commitment survives** the regrounding (a fix:
before, regrounding a canonical decision silently dropped its commitment).

## The development history

The ledger is append-only, so it remembers how the canon *settled*:

- **`canon history <id>`** — a decision's grounds and its **commitment trajectory**
  over time (every commit across its lineage, oldest first). In the editor, **`h`**
  in the Canon dashboard renders it into the Thoughts pane.
- **`canon log`** — every commitment event across the ledger, oldest first: the
  story's canon settling, draft by draft.

## The pre-cut guard

Delete a paragraph that **established** canon decisions and the confirmation warns
first — naming the decisions and how many others rest on them ("2 canon decision(s)
were established here… — 3 rest on it"). It's advisory (it informs, never blocks),
and since the ledger is derived and separate, deleting the prose keeps the decisions
rather than pruning them. *"What breaks if I cut this?"* lands at the moment of the
cut.

## Scripting

`ink.canon.history` `( id -- list )` joins the read-only Bund surface
(`ink.canon.{list, impact, why, history, forks}`). The writes — `commit`,
`ground`/`unground`, and the author-confirmed harvest — stay on the CLI and in the
editor, the same discipline the other intelligences keep.

---

## Upgrade notes

Nothing to migrate. On an existing 3.11.0 ledger the deterministic grounding applies
to decisions recorded from now on; to draw edges among decisions already recorded,
re-run `canon harvest`/`accept` for a scope, or `canon ground` by hand. The default
Gemini model id fix from 3.11.0 still applies.
