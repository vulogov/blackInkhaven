# Inkhaven 1.6.7 — Grounded Readers

_The Inner family learns the world. The two AI readers that used to read your prose
blind now read it against everything you have already declared — your world, your
characters, your symbols, your timeline — and a new reader joins them. Plus a
whole-book haiku, three more languages for it, a new companion book, and a sweep of
small correctness fixes. No new runtime crates._

## The Inner family, grounded (INNER-GROUND-1)

Until now, two of Inkhaven's AI readers read your manuscript with no knowledge of
the world behind it. That changes here.

- **Inner Socrates and Inner Editor now open grounded.** A new shared module reads
  what you have declared — the cast and their arcs (Characters), the symbol and
  motif library (Mythology), the world's open coherence tensions, and the story
  timeline — and hands it to the reader as a preamble, so a Socratic question or a
  craft note is asked _against your world_ rather than in a vacuum. Each source
  self-disables when nothing is declared, so a bare project behaves exactly as
  before. The grounding preamble is localised to the project language (EN / RU / FR
  / DE / ES).
- **A new persona — the Inner Historian.** A Question-stance reader that presses the
  page for _chronological and historical consistency_ against the world you built:
  whether an event's timing fits the established order, whether elapsed time is
  consistent, whether a remembered past matches the record. Choose it from the
  Inner Socrates roster (`Ctrl+B J`).
- **The world-coherence loop is closed.** The Inner Theologian's whole-book scan now
  writes its findings into the world model's reserved `theological` domain, so both
  the theologian's own grounding and the utopian-architect persona light up on a
  channel that was always empty before.

## A haiku for the whole book (HAIKU-3)

The zero-AI Output-pane haiku can now reflect the _whole manuscript_ rather than the
paragraph under the cursor. Set `editor.haiku_scope: "book"` and the poem is chosen
by a centroid over a spread sample of your book — cached and rebuilt only when a
paragraph is added or removed, so it stays cheap. And the haiku speaks three more
languages: **Portuguese, Italian, and Japanese** join the existing five. Still no AI
API, no network — the poems are baked into the binary and selected by the local
embedding model.

## A new companion book — _Developing a story with Inkhaven_

A fifth book joins the shelf: a process guide that sorts everything Inkhaven
supports into eight **tracks** — fiction, utopia, science fiction, nonfiction,
scenarios, technical, scientific, and theology/philosophy — with a full working
guide for each, hands-on command-level procedures, and two appendices (a complete
keybinding reference and a grouped CLI reference). It answers the one question the
feature manuals don't: _which of these tools are mine, and in what order, for the
book I am actually writing?_

## Hardening and correctness

A round of the deferred audit tail, each with a regression test:

- **Research / sources:** BibTeX import tolerates whitespace before the brace; a
  `null` field no longer drops a whole citation entry; LLM-reply JSON extraction is
  brace-balanced (a trailing `{…}` in prose no longer dumps the raw reply as the
  fact); corrupt verdict / import sidecars are backed up rather than silently reset;
  a swallowed citation write now surfaces; `/verify` is capped; RAG passages are
  clipped; `/chain` stops on a failed step instead of feeding the error forward.
- **ConLang import:** PolyGlot resolves part-of-speech in a genuine second pass (a
  word before the type table keeps its POS); Toolbox prefers the gloss over the
  definition regardless of field order.
- **Robustness:** a recursion depth-guard on the Facts tree walk.

## Dependencies & compatibility

**No new runtime crates.** New config `editor.haiku_scope` (`"paragraph"` default |
`"book"`); the grounding and the new persona are additive and self-disabling. All
existing projects are unaffected. Test suite → 2329.
