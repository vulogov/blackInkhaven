# Inkhaven 1.8.0 — The Linguistic Layer

_The flagship that consolidates the entire 1.7 series. Over twenty-four point
releases Inkhaven grew from a constructed-language tool into a full **general
linguistics workbench** — one that still builds imaginary languages beautifully, but
now models, measures, and interrogates real ones just as well. 1.8.0 is that story
told whole, plus a brand-new companion book that puts the workbench to work on a
living language. **No new runtime crates.**_

## What the linguistic layer is

Everything the `inkhaven language` family does, gathered into one arc. The machinery
never cared whether the language it was handed was invented or inherited — a phoneme
inventory is a phoneme inventory, a corpus is a corpus, an interlinear gloss glosses
whatever you give it — and 1.8.0 is where that stops being an accident and becomes the
point.

### Phonology — the sound system, measured

_(1.7.0 The Linguist's Desk · 1.7.1 The Phonologist's Toolkit · 1.7.3 The Analyst's
Bench · 1.7.4 Rounding Out the Sounds)_

Declare a phoneme inventory and the workbench reports on it: size and balance
(`metrics` — entropy, evenness, the Zipf fit, syllable saturation, mora weight), the
contrasts a language leans on (`pairs` — minimal pairs and functional load), how
ordinary the inventory is against a distinctive-feature matrix (`naturalness`), and
where each segment lives in the word (`distribution`). Works over any script; the
feature tools key off the IPA, and the new companion book shows exactly how to bridge
an orthographic model to a phonemic one.

### Morphology — words taken apart

_(1.7.11 Partial Reduplication · 1.7.12 Ablaut)_

The morphological parser handles the hard cases real languages throw at it — partial
reduplication and ablaut (root-internal vowel alternation) join affixation and full
reduplication — so a parser can recognise a stem however the grammar has reshaped it.

### Syntax — the structure of a clause

_(1.7.5 The Engine Begins · 1.7.6 The Syntax Engine · 1.7.7 The Engine in the
Companion · 1.7.8–1.7.10 The Oracle · 1.7.13 Agreement, Generalised)_

A genuine syntax engine: X-bar phrase-structure trees, movement (wh-fronting, head
movement), and binding. The clause **Oracle** checks a sentence against the grammar
you declared — argument structure from each verb's valence, and agreement between any
head and its dependent, not just subject and verb — and it rides along in the review
pass so structural problems surface as you write.

### Interlinear glossed text — the annotation workbench

_(1.7.14 Interlinear Glossed Text · 1.7.15 The Segmentation Line · 1.7.16 Stored Texts
· 1.7.17 Texts to LaTeX · 1.7.18 Curating the Translation)_

Turn a sentence into a Leipzig-style interlinear gloss — surface, morpheme
segmentation, gloss, and free translation — auto-built from the lexicon and
morphology, editable by hand, saved with the project, and exportable to publication-
ready LaTeX.

### Corpus — the language as it is used

_(1.7.19 The Corpus Engine · 1.7.20 Collocation · 1.7.21 The Corpus Reads Your Book)_

Build a corpus from your stored interlinears or straight from your manuscript prose
and query it: `frequency` (with lemma-folding that reunites an inflectional paradigm),
`concordance` (keyword-in-context), and `collocations` (habitual word partners ranked
by pointwise mutual information). The corpus reads real text, including the book you
are writing.

### Hypotheses — claims you can test

_(1.7.2 The Consequence Tracer · 1.7.22 The Hypothesis Register · 1.7.23 Testing a
Hypothesis)_

State a claim about a language — a sound change, a cognate set, a borrowing — in a
persistent register, attach evidence, and test it: trace a proposed sound change's
consequences across the lexicon and move a hypothesis from *proposed* to *supported*
or *refuted* on what you find.

## Two companion books

- **Developing a Constructed Language** now covers the full toolset — new chapters on
  the syntax engine, the clause Oracle, and interlinear glossed text, plus a typed-
  grammar reference for verb classes and universal-grammar parameters. Stamped to 1.8.0.
- **Linguistic Research with Inkhaven** _(new)_ — the mirror image. It takes a language
  you did **not** invent — Russian — and studies it end to end with the same commands:
  its sound system, its fusional morphology, the structure of its clauses, a corpus
  drawn from real (public-domain) texts, its place on the typological map, and its
  history. Written in English, Russian on every page as the worked example, no
  linguistics degree assumed.

## Hardening

_(1.7.24 Pre-1.8 Hardening)_ — the layer's store-writes go through `io_atomic`, the
companion caches its corpus per session, and minimal-pairs collection is memory-
bounded. Nothing changed for the user; the internals came up to the project's
stability bar.

## Dependencies & compatibility

**No new runtime crates** across the entire 1.7 series or in this consolidation. Every
command listed above already shipped and is unchanged; 1.8.0 adds the new companion
book and the flagship framing, nothing else. Warning-free (binary and tests).
