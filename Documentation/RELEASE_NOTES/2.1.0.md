# Inkhaven 2.1.0 — CHORUS: Voice & Style at Book Scale

_Inkhaven has long measured your **narrator's** voice — sentence rhythm, lexical
diversity, hedging, interiority (NARR-1). 2.1 measures the rest of what makes a
book's voice hold together: does each **character** sound like themselves and
distinct from the others, and does the manuscript keep its discipline — one POV
per scene, a consistent tense, a stable register?_

The flagship of 2.1.0 is **CHORUS** — voice & style at book scale. Its thesis:

> **NARR-1 profiles the narrator. CHORUS profiles the cast, enforces the rules of
> the game, and coaches.**

Three measurement pillars, synthesised by a new inner-family reader — all
**advisory**: CHORUS measures and reports, it never edits your prose. It builds
almost entirely on the metric engine and dialogue attribution already in-tree, so
it adds **no new runtime crates**.

## The cast (Pillar A)

- **Character voice fingerprints** — each character's attributed dialogue profiled
  with the *same* metric engine that profiles the narrator.
- **The distinctiveness matrix** — do any two characters read *identically*? Each
  voice is z-scored across the cast (genre-relative — the baseline is your own
  book's spread) and compared; look-alike pairs are flagged. *"Mara and Joren read
  identically"* — the classic revision-stage flaw, with no existing detector.
- **Per-character drift** — does a character sound like themselves across their
  arc (measured against their first chapter)?

Sparse speakers are profiled but never *flagged* — CHORUS reports a confidence and
refuses to judge a voice it can't measure. `inkhaven chorus voices`.

## The discipline (Pillar B)

- **Head-hopping** — a named character other than the scene's POV shown accessing
  their own inner life (`Joren wondered…` in a Mara-POV scene). Declare a scene's
  POV with a `pov:<name>` / `pov:first` / `pov:omniscient` paragraph tag, or it's
  inferred.
- **Tense slips** — a manuscript that lapses out of its established tense.

`inkhaven chorus scan`.

### The honest language gate

Tense-slip detection is **English-only, and Russian is excluded by design.**
Russian narrative tense is governed by *aspect* — the historical present and
perfective/imperfective interleaving are legitimate devices, not slips — and
nothing in the tree models aspect, so a past→present heuristic would be *wrong*
for Russian. CHORUS says so plainly rather than false-flagging. Character voice
and head-hop **do** work in Russian.

## The register (Pillar C)

The narrator's **register** — contraction rate, archaism, formality, latinate
diction — tracked per chapter so *drift* becomes visible ("the prose gets casual
in Act III"). Solid for EN/RU; sparser languages degrade to what their lists
cover rather than guessing.

## The Inner Stylist — the coach

The **seventh inner-family reader** (alongside the Editor, Socrates, Theologian,
Poet, Rigor, Grounding). It doesn't measure — it **synthesises**: it reads all
three pillars and turns the numbers into a few grounded **Praise / Note /
Concern** observations, and — on the slow track — LLM coaching in the
inner-family voice (*"I notice…"*, never a rewrite).

- **In the editor** — the Inner Stylist rides the **`Ctrl+B Shift+C`** review pass
  (its observations land in the Output pane), and the family hub
  **`Ctrl+B J → Y`** runs it on demand.
- **On the CLI** — `chorus stylist` (Praise/Note/Concern, each with a stable
  suppression key), `--coach` (grounded LLM coaching), `--suppress <key>`
  (silence a finding, persisted in `inner_stylist.db`), and `chorus report` (the
  one-screen dashboard: narrator + cast + distinctiveness + Stylist).

## Configuration

```hjson
chorus:  { distinct_threshold: 0.5, distinct_ignore_pairs: ["Mara|Joren"], register_drift_threshold: 0.08 }
stylist: { enabled: true, session_budget: 0.15 }
```

All knobs inform and cap — per Inkhaven's permissive principle, they never block.
