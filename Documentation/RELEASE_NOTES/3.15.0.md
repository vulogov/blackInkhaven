# Inkhaven 3.15.0 — Canon in Hand

_3.14.0 put the Canon Ledger in view while you write. 3.15.0 lets you **tend it
there**: set how settled a decision is, review what the model proposed, and
harvest the open paragraph — all from the Canon pane, without a trip to the shell.
A second track, **A World That Keeps Its Word**, makes the worldbuilder and
`realworld` do what their schema and docs already said they did. No new store,
no new dependency; warning-free._

Full guides: [`CANON.md`](../CANON.md) · [`WORLDBUILDING.md`](../WORLDBUILDING.md).

---

## Canon in Hand

### Set commitment where you read it — `c`

`c` on a decision, in the Canon pane or the Canon dashboard, opens a picker over
**floated → drafted → committed → canonical → retconned** with the current level
marked and a line on what each means. `Enter` records it as the author (the same
write as `canon commit`). If the choice leaves something firm resting on something
soft, the status line says so *at that moment* — from either side: this decision
rests on a weaker one, or a stronger one rests on it.

### Review proposals beside the prose — `a` / `x` / `A`

What `canon harvest` staged for the open paragraph is listed in the pane under its
live decisions as `? [kind] gist` rows, with the grounds each *would* rest on. `a`
accepts the one under the cursor, `x` discards it, `A` accepts all of this
paragraph's. The title counts them; proposals staged for other paragraphs show as
one count line. It is the same confirmation as `canon accept` — literally one
shared code path — at the grain of a single proposal.

### Harvest the open paragraph — `H`

One model call, in the project language, off the UI thread (`⠋ harvesting` in the
title, `Esc` cancels). The status line states the size of the call before it runs.
The result is **staged only** — nothing enters the ledger until your `a` — and a
proposal that repeats what the paragraph already has, or what is already staged,
is dropped, so harvesting twice does not stage twice. It reads the saved text.
(History in the pane is now lowercase `h` only.)

### The riders

- **Impact on edit.** Saving a paragraph that established decisions *other*
  decisions rest on says so once per paragraph per session — the changing-it half
  of the delete guard. Nothing is blocked.
- **Commitment at a glance.** The `◈` in the Tree and Outline is tinted by the
  strongest commitment among the paragraph's decisions: dim (unmarked / floated),
  plain (drafted / committed), **bold** (canonical), struck (all retconned).
- The open-paragraph summary now counts only decisions *elsewhere* that rest on
  the paragraph's, not its own decisions resting on one another.

## A World That Keeps Its Word

### One ruler

Population was computed on a fixed 625 km² cell while distance and climate
treated the same grid as the whole planet. Every layer now measures a cell the
same way. An Earth-like world keeps its bronze-age ~38 million (the starter world
is unchanged), but population now follows the planet's radius — twice the radius,
about four times the people — and the latitude of the land.

**`geology.dem.scale_km_per_pixel` is now optional, and used.** Declared, the
heightmap is a *region* of that size: journeys, trade routes and population all
follow. Omitted, the image is the whole planet. `validate` prints the declared
scale, and warns when a regional map's climate still runs pole to pole.

### The interview speaks your language

The guided interview asks in the project language — en / ru / fr / de / es — and
takes answers in it: `оранжевая`, `naine rouge`, `roter Zwerg`; `да`, `oui`, `ja`;
`0,6`; `древние`, `uralt`. A class letter typed on a Cyrillic layout is read as
the Latin one it looks like. `/orogeny` is the new command behind the mountains
question. The slow fact-check and coherence passes now explain their findings in
the project language.

### The rest

- **The week is used.** `calendar.weekdays` / `day_names` date a day in
  `realworld weather`, `scene`, `calendar` and the `Ctrl+B W` scene header —
  "day 2 of Frostmoon · Fourday".
- **The worldbuilder sees your heightmap.** The live score, `/compile`,
  `/validate` and the Map pane compiled a procedural twin of a heightmap world;
  they now read the heightmap, and report one that cannot be read.
- **Compiled once.** The worldbuilder compiled the world separately for the score
  and for the map on every change; the idle fact-check recompiled geology on every
  run. Both now reuse one compile.
- **Small promises.** Terrain noise uses the whole seed (seeds differing only
  above bit 32 no longer share terrain); positions off the map are warned about
  instead of silently clamped; a landmark on a capital's cell is reported; a
  planet with no axial tilt no longer has a "deep winter"; `/terrain` names its
  heightmap by content, so a sculpt never overwrites a committed one, and
  `/undo` / `/reset` clean up after it.

## Upgrade notes

- **Population figures can change** for worlds that are not Earth-sized, and
  somewhat for other seeds. Re-run `realworld compile --materialize` to refresh
  the World book.
- **A heightmap that declares `scale_km_per_pixel` is now a region.** Its
  distances and population shrink to the declared size. If the image is the whole
  world, remove the key.
- Nothing else to migrate. The Canon pane's new keys only act when pressed; the
  model still only proposes.

## Known limits

A regional map's latitude is not re-anchored (its climate still spans pole to
pole). The `propose` family's prose and rationales remain English. Whole-chapter
harvest stays a shell job (`canon harvest <scope>`).
