# Inkhaven 1.8.2 — Scansion

_Read the metre of a line of poetry. `inkhaven language scan` marks each syllable
strong or weak and names the foot — iambic tetrameter, trochaic trimeter — building on
the phonology layer's syllabifier and stress model. Handles languages whose stress is
lexical (Russian above all) through a three-link resolution chain. **No new runtime
crates.**_

## Verse scansion

`inkhaven language scan <lang> --text "…"` scans one line or a whole stanza:

```
  У | лу ко мор ья | дуб | зе лё ный
  · | ×  ×   /  ×  |  /  | ×  /   ×
  → iambic tetrameter · 9 syllable(s) · fit 0.88
```

Each word is syllabified and its beats marked (`/` stressed, `×` unstressed, `·`
flexible); the line's pattern is matched against the metrical feet (iamb, trochee,
anapest, dactyl, amphibrach) and reported as, e.g., *iambic tetrameter*. A multi-line
text also gets a dominant-metre summary. Nuances the tool gets right:

- **Feminine endings** — a rising line with an extra unstressed syllable (9 syllables of
  iambic verse) is tetrameter *with a feminine ending*, not pentameter.
- **Flexible monosyllables** — a one-syllable word with no fixed stress promotes or
  demotes to fit the metre, exactly as scansion by hand allows.

## Stress that isn't rule-governed

Scansion needs to know where each word is stressed, and for many languages a fixed rule
won't do — Russian stress is lexical, mobile, and contrastive (за́мок "castle" vs замо́к
"lock"). Stress is resolved per word through a **three-link chain**:

1. an explicit **combining-acute mark** in the text (`лу́`), the way Russian verse is
   annotated;
2. the lexicon's new **`stress`** field — `inkhaven language add-word … --stress 2`
   records the stressed syllable of an irregular word;
3. the language's **stress rule** (initial / penult / Latin …) as the last resort.

The whole path is language-independent: a conlang with a fixed rule scans automatically,
while a natural language with lexical stress uses the marks or the lexicon.

## Dependencies & compatibility

**No new runtime crates.** Scansion composes the existing syllabifier and stress
evaluator; the only surface additions are the `language scan` command, an optional
`stress` lexicon field, and `add-word --stress`. Purely additive, warning-free (binary
and tests). Test suite → 2595.
