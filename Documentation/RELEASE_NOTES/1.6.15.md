# Inkhaven 1.6.15 — Scientific Papers

_The desk gains what a journal article needs: a title block, a journal LaTeX target,
an IMRaD scaffold, cross-references you can both insert and see break, a double-blind
submission build, and a package picker for the Typst Universe. No new runtime crates._

## The paper front matter

A paper is not only its prose — a journal expects a title block. Declare it once in a
`frontmatter` config block — **abstract**, **keywords**, **authors** with
**affiliations**, **ORCID**, corresponding author, and **funding** — and it renders
into the title page for the `pdf`, `tex`, and `typst` exports. Authors sharing an
affiliation share one superscript; the corresponding author is marked and their
address noted. The labels (_Abstract_, _Keywords_, _Corresponding author_, _Funding_)
follow the book's language — first-class in English, Russian, French, German, and
Spanish. Leave the block empty and nothing renders, so a book that is not a paper is
byte-for-byte unchanged.

## A journal LaTeX target

`inkhaven export tex` already produced a standalone `article`. Now it targets a
**journal document class** when you name one: `tex_export.document_class` (e.g.
`IEEEtran`, `elsarticle`) with `class_options` (`conference`, `twocolumn`, …) and
`extra_packages`. Inkhaven rewrites the converter's document-class line to the class
you asked for and folds in the packages the venue needs, de-duplicating any the
converter already emits. Left unset, the export is unchanged.

## The scientific template

`inkhaven init --template scientific` scaffolds the track in one step — an **IMRaD**
manuscript (Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion), the academic genre set, a
seeded methodology paragraph, and a post-init message pointing at the front-matter and
journal-class knobs.

## Cross-references, both ways

Referencing a label that does not exist — `@fig:flux` with no `<fig:flux>` — is a hard
Typst error that halts the build and is easy to miss in a wall of compiler output. When
a build fails on one, Inkhaven now lifts it out as a first-class **cross-reference
finding** in the Output pane (kind `xref`), naming the label and the `file:line:col`,
so a dangling `@fig:`/`@eq:`/`@tbl:` is as findable as an uncited claim. Works for both
compile engines. And to stop writing a dangling reference in the first place, `Ctrl+V &`
opens a **cross-reference picker** — every label defined across the manuscript,
fuzzy-searchable — and inserts the matching `@label` at the cursor.

## A double-blind submission

`export tex --blind` (also `--blind` on the `pdf`/`typst` export) renders the title
block with the **identifying front matter withheld** — authors, affiliations, ORCID,
funding — while keeping the title, abstract, keywords, and availability statements a
reviewer still needs. Those statements are front matter of their own: `data_availability`
and `code_availability` render as labelled blocks (multilingual) and, unlike the author
line, survive `--blind`.

## Reach the Typst Universe

`Ctrl+V #` opens a **package import picker**: fuzzy-find a Typst Universe package by
name and Inkhaven inserts its `#import "@preview/…": *` line at the cursor. The
catalogue is fetched once (via a crate HTTP client — no external binary) and cached
under `.inkhaven/` on a configurable TTL, with a stale-copy fallback when offline;
`Ctrl+R` inside the picker forces a refresh, and `typst_universe.url` points it at a
different index.

## Dependencies & compatibility

**No new runtime crates.** New config `frontmatter` (incl. `data_availability` /
`code_availability`), `tex_export`, `typst_universe`; new template `scientific`; new
chords `Ctrl+V #` (package import) and `Ctrl+V &` (cross-reference); new export flag
`--blind`; new Output-pane kind `xref`. Everything is additive — existing projects are
unaffected. Test suite → 2395.
