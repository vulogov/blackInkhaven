# Inkhaven 1.7.17 — Texts to LaTeX

_Stored interlinears can now leave the tool in the form linguists actually publish.
`texts --format latex` renders them as a `linguex` LaTeX document — each text a
numbered example with the segmented sentence over its gloss and a free translation,
ready to paste into a grammar sketch or a paper. No new runtime crates._

## Exporting interlinears

`texts --format latex` emits a `linguex` document of the stored texts (one, or all):

```sh
inkhaven language texts Eldar --format latex            # every stored text
inkhaven language texts Eldar --name the-oath-1 --format latex   # just one
```

Each text becomes a numbered `\ex.` with a `\gll` — the morpheme-segmented sentence
over its gloss, word-aligned — and a `\glt` free translation:

```latex
\ex.
\gll kata-t nilo\\
stone-DAT nilo\\
\glt `stone friend'
```

LaTeX specials are escaped, and a multi-word gloss keeps `\gll`'s two lines aligned
by binding it with a non-breaking space (`old~man`). The default `--format text`
still prints the aligned terminal block.

## The Annotation Workbench

Four of five slices are in: auto-gloss, the segmentation line, persistence, and now
export. A TUI editing surface is what remains, and the stored `Texts` are the
substrate the coming Corpus Engine will index.

## Dependencies & compatibility

**No new runtime crates.** Additive — a new `interchange::igt_linguex` renderer and a
`--format` option on the `texts` verb; the shared per-word columns moved onto
`Igt::columns` (used by both the text and LaTeX renderings), with no change to
existing output. Warning-free (binary and tests). Test suite → 2569.
