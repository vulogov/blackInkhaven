# Inkhaven 1.6.20 — The Rigor Reader

_A deterministic reader for the shape of your argument. Where the Inner Theologian
reads a passage for its moral weight, the reasoning-rigor reader reads it for its
validity — whether the argument, on its own terms, actually follows. Zero-AI,
multilingual, advisory. No new runtime crates._

## The reasoning-rigor reader

A new deterministic, zero-AI reader flags five recurring shapes of a weak argument,
each by the cue word that gives it away:

- **false dichotomy** — a forced binary that hides a third option ("either … or",
  "the only alternative")
- **question-begging** — a claim smuggled in as self-evident ("obviously", "of
  course", "everyone knows")
- **straw man** — a view answered in its weaker form ("so-called", "would have us
  believe")
- **overgeneralization** — a strong absolute a single counterexample would break
  ("always", "never", "without exception")
- **non-sequitur** — a conclusion connective ("therefore", "thus") with no warrant
  marker ("because", "since") anywhere in the paragraph

It is the argument-side complement to the Inner Theologian's moral reader: it does
not judge whether you are right, only whether the prose has slipped into a form a
reader who wanted to break it would seize on. Every finding is **advisory, never a
verdict**.

## Multilingual

The reader is fully multilingual — English, Russian, French, German, and Spanish —
with conservative cue lists **and localized advisories** per language. A Russian
argument is read against Russian cues and the advisory comes back in Russian
(«очевидно» → question-begging, «следовательно» → non-sequitur). The marker language
follows the project `language`, overridable with `rigor.language`.

## Three surfaces

- **In the editor:** `Ctrl+B J → R` reads the open paragraph and drops anchored ⊬
  advisories into the Output pane — synchronous and instant, since it is zero-AI.
- **In the review pass:** the reader runs alongside the theologian and myth checks,
  emitting anchored findings across the book.
- **From the shell:** `inkhaven rigor scan` runs it as a pre-submission pass, with
  `--signal <category>` to focus on one failure, `--json` for tooling, and `--strict`
  to fail a continuous-integration step on any hit (advisory — exit 0 — otherwise).

Each category has its own toggle (`rigor.false_dichotomy`, `rigor.overgeneralization`,
…), so a genre that uses a construction legitimately — a rhetorical "never" — can mute
just that signal.

## Documentation

The _Theology and Philosophy with Inkhaven_ companion manual gains a "Read the
argument for rigor" section in its reading chapter: the three manuscript readers now
divide an editor's labour cleanly — confront asks _is it true to the sources?_, the
Inner Theologian _does it carry its weight?_, and the rigor reader _does it follow?_

## Dependencies & compatibility

**No new runtime crates.** New command `inkhaven rigor scan`; new editor chord
`Ctrl+B J → R`; new config `rigor.*` (enabled by default, per-category toggles).
Everything is additive and advisory — existing projects are unaffected. Test suite →
2455.
