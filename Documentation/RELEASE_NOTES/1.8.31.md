# Inkhaven 1.8.31 — The Review Queue

_RESRCH-6 completes its trust loop. Autonomous research (1.8.26–1.8.29) emits its findings as
**untrusted** Facts; this release adds the human end of that loop — an interactive queue that
gathers them and walks the author through triage, one claim at a time._

## `/review` — triaging untrusted, agentic-emitted facts

In the Research Assistant (`inkhaven research`), the new `/review` command opens a queue over
the facts an `--agentic` run left behind — the exact set of "the machine said this; have you
checked?":

- **The queue is narrow by design.** A fact appears only when all three hold: its provenance
  *thread* is `agentic` (emitted by an autonomous run, not authored, promoted, or pulled from a
  structured source); it has not already been *accepted*; and it is not marked *undisputed*.
  Your own work, promoted facts, and structured sources never clutter it.
- **Three one-key verdicts.** Move the cursor with `j`/`k` (or the arrows); act with a single
  key. `a` **accept** — you believe it; the fact is tagged `fact:reviewed` and leaves the queue
  for good, even across sessions. `d` **delete** — it's wrong or noise; removed from the Facts
  book. `u` **undisputed** — it's an authorial premise; tagged `fact:undisputed` and excluded
  from `/factcheck`. Whichever you press, the fact drops out and the cursor advances; `Esc`
  stops without losing your place.
- **Contradictions first.** A `≠` flag marks any queued fact caught in a recorded contradiction
  (the in-loop gate from 1.8.28), and the detail pane points at `/contradict` — pulling the
  claims that most need scrutiny to the front so attention lands where the risk is.

Accepting records *triage state*, not trust tier: a reviewed model fact is still a model fact.
To raise a fact's tier, reach for `/upgrade` as before. The queue is about attention, not
about promoting closed-world claims to grounded ones.

`/review` is the human end of the autonomous loop. The chain now reads cleanly: `--agentic`
*emits* untrusted facts and *flags* the contradictions among them; `/review` *gathers* those
facts, *surfaces* the flags, and *records the author's verdict* on each.
