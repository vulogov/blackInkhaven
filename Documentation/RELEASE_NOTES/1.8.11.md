# Inkhaven 1.8.11 — The Inner Poet (POEM-3, PO-P5 fast track)

_The fifth Inner-family reader arrives. Stand on a verse paragraph, press **Ctrl+B J → P**,
and the Inner Poet scans its metre and rhyme against the declared `poem:` form — findings
to the Output pane, deterministic and offline. It composes all four POEM engines into the
first reader that actually reads a poem. **No new runtime crates.**_

## The Inner Poet

`Ctrl+B J → P` on a `para:verse-*` paragraph runs the fast track: for each line it checks
the metre against the form's declared foot (feminine endings and catalexis noted, not
flagged), and for the stanza it checks every rhyming pair the scheme calls for. Findings
land in the Output pane under a new `♪` category (`poem`), anchored to the paragraph — a
`Concern` for a badly-overrun line or a broken rhyme, a `Note` for a near-rhyme or a metre
departure, and — unusually for an Inner reader — **praise** for a line that scans cleanly.

The declared form is read from a `poem:` HJSON block among the paragraph's siblings (or at
its parent's level), so a stanza carries its own metre, foot count, and rhyme scheme. The
Inner Poet **never rewrites** — it observes and reports, exactly like the Inner Editor.

## Composing the engines

This is where POEM-1 through POEM-4 come together: the `poem:` form (1.8.7) declares the
target, the syllabifier (1.8.8) and the metre scanner (1.8.9, itself the reused 1.8.2
scansion engine) check each line, and the rhyme engine (1.8.10) checks the scheme. The pure
scan is also available from the CLI:

```sh
inkhaven poetry scan --form shakespearean_sonnet --language en --text "…"
```

## What's next
