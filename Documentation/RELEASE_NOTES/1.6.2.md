# Inkhaven 1.6.2 — The Woven World

_A worldbuilding-polish release that weaves the deterministic World simulation
into the rest of the project. The world can now propose Mythology symbols,
realm rulers as Characters, and (with an AI reading) critique itself into your
Notes; any Place — even one you wrote by hand — can be positioned for the map.
The book gains a Mythology chapter and a clearer opening. No new runtime crates._

## World → Mythology (MYTH-2)

- **`inkhaven realworld propose-myth`** reads each culture's *belief* (a
  sky-pantheon, ancestor veneration, a cult of the seasons…) and proposes a
  Mythology entry — a `para:myth-symbol` or `para:myth-motif` block — that you
  accept into the Mythology book like any Place proposal. Deterministic (a fixed
  belief→seed table + a generic fallback), so it stays reproducible across
  `realworld variants`; peoples who share a belief merge into one entry, credited
  in its `traditions`.
- **The Mythology book now teaches its own schema.** A freshly-created project
  seeds it with a short guide and one worked example symbol (only a symbol — it
  raises no false `myth check` finding on an empty manuscript), so you no longer
  open a blank page.

## World → Characters (WORLD-12)

- **`inkhaven realworld propose-rulers`** proposes one ruler per realm — a
  Character stub named in the world's own style and rooted in the realm's ethos
  and belief — for you to accept into the Characters book. The world models no one
  else: one rooted figure per realm, offered for you to rename and flesh out.

## World → Places, onto the map (WORLD-12)

- **`inkhaven realworld set-coords <name>`** gives any Place a location on the
  world grid — by geographic degrees (`--lat`/`--lon`) or grid cells
  (`--x`/`--y`) — and fills the biome under the cell from the compiled climate.
  Previously only compiler-born Places had coordinates; now a Place you authored
  by hand draws and round-trips on the plakat map exactly like any other. The map
  emits a labeled marker for **every** coordinate-bearing Place, not only those
  sitting on a compiler settlement.

## An AI reading of your world (WORLD-12)

- **`inkhaven realworld critique`** runs the free deterministic lints, then asks a
  model to judge the whole world's *consistency* (a declared value that fights its
  compiled consequence) and *plausibility* (numbers that would not produce the
  world described), returning ranked, concrete recommendations. **`--write-notes`**
  files each into the Notes book; **`--lints-only`** skips the AI. It never edits
  `world.hjson` — advisory only. Cost-capped on the world slow-track budget (the
  cap informs, never blocks); tuned by a new `world:` config block
  (`critique_enabled`, `critique_max_tokens`, `critique_cost_warn`).

## Building the World with Inkhaven — the book

- A new chapter, **"Myth and Belief"** (Part V), carries a culture's belief into
  the Mythology book — symbols, motifs, archetypes wired to your characters, and
  the `myth scan` / `myth check` honesty passes.
- The introduction now leads with the idea that Inkhaven is **one environment, not
  a toolbox**, and emphasizes that your **Characters, Places, and Artefacts books
  are yours to author by hand** — the World simulation is one contributor that
  *proposes* into them, never their author.
- New sections cover `propose-rulers` (ch. 10), `realworld critique` (ch. 12), and
  `set-coords` (ch. 17), plus command-reference rows.

## Dependencies & compatibility

**No new runtime crates.** New commands: `realworld propose-myth`,
`realworld propose-rulers`, `realworld set-coords`, `realworld critique`; the
world proposal queue is now multi-book (Places · Mythology · Characters); new
`world:` config block; the Mythology system book seeds an example on first
creation. All additive — existing projects are unaffected.
