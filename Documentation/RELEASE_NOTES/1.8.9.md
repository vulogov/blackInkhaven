# Inkhaven 1.8.9 — The Metre Scanner (POEM-5, PO-P3)

_Scan a line of verse: mark its beats, detect its metre, and check it against a declared
form. This reuses the scansion engine shipped in 1.8.2 — now fed by the natural-language
syllabifier — so the poetry layer gets metre analysis without a second engine. **No new
runtime crates.**_

## Scanning a line

`inkhaven poetry metre --line "…"` builds the beat sequence, detects the metre, and — with
`--form` — checks the line against that form's declared metre:

```sh
inkhaven poetry metre --line "У лукомо́рья ду́б зелё́ный" --language ru
#   · × × / × / × / ×   (9 syllables)
#   → detected: iambic tetrameter (fit 0.88)
```

Stress per word is taken from an explicit acute mark in the text where present (the way
Russian verse is annotated), else the syllabifier's rule; a monosyllabic word is *flexible*
and may promote or demote to fit the metre. A rising line with one extra unstressed syllable
is reported as a **feminine ending**, not a defect.

## One engine, two front ends

The metre logic is the scansion engine from **1.8.2** (`src/conlang/scansion.rs`) — its foot
templates, feminine-ending handling, and best-fit detection. Rather than build a second
scanner, POEM-5 exposes that engine's beat→metre core and feeds it beats from the POEM-2
natural-language syllabifier. `src/poetry/metre.rs` adds the poetry-facing surface: a `Foot`
parsed from a `poem:` block, a declared-metre check (with feminine ending / catalexis), the
syllabic and accentual traditions, and a free-verse profiler.

Accuracy note: Russian verse with stress marks scans accurately; English is rougher until
the lexical stress dictionary lands (a later refinement — rule-based English stress
mis-places some polysyllables).

## Dependencies & compatibility

**No new runtime crates.** Reuses the existing scansion engine and the POEM-2 syllabifier;
adds `src/poetry/metre.rs` and a `poetry metre` diagnostic command. Warning-free (binary and
tests). Test suite → 2632.
